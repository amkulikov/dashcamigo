          FAQ

          Short answers

          dashcamigo is free — how can I help?
          We don't need donations. If dashcamigo helped, star it on GitHub. If you found it through a forum, social media or chat, go back and say what you thought. That helps more people find the project.
          Is there a dashcam app for PC, or do I need the vendor's app?
          You don't need a separate app. dashcamigo opens 70mai, BlackVue, Viofo and other recordings right in your browser — a free alternative to the vendor's app, with nothing to install and no account. You can also install it from the browser — it pins to your dock and works offline.
          My dashcam splits every drive into 1‑3 minute clips — can I watch them as one trip?
          Yes — automatically. Drop the SD‑card folder and the clips join into continuous trips: one timeline, one route on the map, playback that doesn't stop at file boundaries. Need a single file? Select a range — even one spanning several clips — and save it as one MP4.
          Which dashcams are supported?
          70mai, Viofo, BlackVue, GoPro, Garmin, Vantrue, Thinkware, Nextbase, FitCamX, Vueroid, Neoline, Juscar, Escort, Carcam, Navitel, SilverStone F1, Roadgid, iBOX, RVMI and others. Anything that writes standard .mp4, .mov or .ts to an SD card opens without setup.
          Are my recordings uploaded anywhere?
          No. dashcamigo opens your recordings right on your device — they are never uploaded anywhere.
          Which devices does it run on?
          Any device with a modern browser: Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iOS. On iOS you need Safari 16+. Can be installed as an app — works offline.
          How much does it cost?
          Free. No ads, no sign-up, no subscriptions, no hidden charges.
          Is dashcamigo open source?
          Yes. The code is public on GitHub: https://github.com/amkulikov/dashcamigo — you can see exactly what the app does with your files, and run your own copy on your computer or server. Either way, your recordings never leave your device.
          Can I cut out a clip and save it as a file?
          Yes. You can trim the timeline, stitch cameras, burn speed, coordinates and a mini-map onto the video, and save as MP4 with GPS inside the file or as a separate .gpx track. A still image saves as a .jpg.
          Can I blur a license plate or a face before sharing?
          Yes. Draw a box over the plate or face — pixelate, a solid fill or a soft blur — and it follows the object as it moves. Like everything else, it happens on your device.
          Which browser works best?
          For the full editor — trim with overlays, crop, change resolution — Chrome, Edge or another Chromium-based browser is best. Firefox and Safari play your recordings, show the map and export the original without setup.
          Switching from RegistratorViewer, Dashcam Viewer or VLC?
          Yes — dashcamigo is a free, in-browser alternative to the usual dashcam tools. See how it compares to RegistratorViewer, Dashcam Viewer, VLC and others — no install, and a map that won't break.

              Files stay on your device

              Your browser may ask something like "Upload files to this site?" - that's its standard folder-access dialog, not what's actually happening. No server. Everything plays right here in the browser.

              Picking a whole folder is slow on iPhone and iPad

              On iPhone and iPad the browser first copies everything in the folder you pick — on a big memory card that takes very long and eats your device's storage. If the copying is interrupted, the copies can get stuck on the device. Picking just the video files you need is much faster. Either way, nothing is uploaded anywhere — everything stays on your device.

              Reading straight from a memory card or USB drive is also very slow on iPhone and iPad — a computer handles it much better. If iPhone or iPad is all you have, first copy the files you need into the Files app and open them from there.

              Reload to switch language?

              Switching language reloads the page, so your loaded recordings will be cleared. You'll need to open them again.

                The map needs WebGL
                
            
                
                
                
                The map is drawn with WebGL, and your browser couldn't start it. Most often WebGL is off because hardware acceleration is disabled.
                
                
                Things to try:
                
                
                  
                    
                • Chrome, Edge or Brave — open chrome://settings/system, switch on "Use graphics acceleration when available", then restart the browser.
                  • 
                    
                • Firefox — Settings → General → Performance, uncheck "Use recommended performance settings", then tick "Hardware acceleration".
                  • 
                    
                • Out of date? Update your browser to the latest version — on a Mac, update macOS too.
                  • 
                    
                • On Linux the browser often blocklists your graphics card — in Chrome open chrome://flags and enable "Override software rendering list", then restart.
                  • 
                
                
                
                Still nothing after a restart? Then your graphics card can't run WebGL — that's fine, video, the speed chart and export still work.
                
                
                
                    Test WebGL at get.webgl.org
